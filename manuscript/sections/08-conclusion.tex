We analyzed K-Waay's stated different-party condition for one \batchreceive
call through a minimal two-slot admission abstraction. Relaxing the condition
permits a receiver-event witness in which one exact sender origin supports
two acceptance occurrences in the same batch context. The auxiliary
message-level control excludes that pattern and satisfies scoped occurrence
injectivity, yet two distinct messages from the same party remain admissible
and accepted. Message distinction and occurrence injectivity therefore do not
substitute for same-batch party distinction under the encoded lifecycle.

The party-level realization supplies the complementary control: its admission
rule constrains the collected party coordinates, its recorded safety result
establishes their distinction at the receiver-event boundary, and a valid
distinct-party batch remains reachable. The resulting design implication is
to state the participant relation required by a batch and assign responsibility
for preserving it, rather than infer that relation from message-level checks.
The evidence ends at model-local \receiveraccept events; extending it to
concrete K-Waay outputs or deployed batching behavior requires additional
justification beyond this analysis.
