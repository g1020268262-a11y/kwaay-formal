\section{Detailed Counterexample Traces}
\label{app:trace-details}

\subsection{Provenance and reading convention}

The sequences below reconstruct the witness shapes reported in Section~5
from the frozen rules and the accompanying execution report:
\begin{center}
  \small\path{docs/rq-v2/prototype-execution-report.md}
\end{center}
They are not exported Tamarin proof graphs,
raw transcripts, or additional prover runs. Network deduction transitions
are suppressed. A displayed rule sequence is one admissible scheduling of
the relevant transitions, not a claim that every execution has that order.
Fresh identifiers may be consistently renamed.

All three variants create parties separately from messages. A Send stores
\texttt{!Sent(A,sid,m)} and exposes the tuple on the network. The batch
rules then collect two tuples under one fresh $(\mathit{bid},\mathit{rst})$.
Admission is distinct from processing: each processing rule requires the
exact persistent origin fact for its own tuple. These facts are reusable,
whereas the slot-processing states are linear.

\subsection{Relaxed exact-origin repetition}

Let $E=(A,\mathit{sid},m)$ be produced by one sender occurrence. The following
sequence explains Figure~\ref{fig:relaxed-counterexample}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{CreateParty} creates $A$; \texttt{SendMessage} emits
    $\mathsf{Send}(A,\mathit{sid},m)@s$, exposes $E$, and stores its origin.
  \item \texttt{CreateBatch} creates $(\mathit{bid},\mathit{rst})$.
    \texttt{CollectSlot1} and \texttt{CollectSlot2} both take the exposed $E$.
  \item \texttt{AdmitRelaxedBatch} emits
    $\mathsf{BatchReceive}(\mathit{bid},\mathit{rst})@b$ with no inequality
    requirement.
  \item \texttt{ProcessSlot1} uses the origin fact and emits
    $\receiveraccept(A,\mathit{sid},m,\mathit{bid},\mathit{rst})@r_1$.
  \item \texttt{ProcessSlot2} reuses that same persistent fact and emits the
    matching event at $r_2$, then reaches \texttt{BatchComplete}.
\end{enumerate}

The recorded existence property requires $s<b<r_1<r_2$ and uniqueness of the
matching Send occurrence for this tuple. It does not exclude unrelated Send
events elsewhere in the trace. The two acceptance occurrences have identical
coordinates but different timepoints. This is the negative instance of the
scoped injectivity property, not a trace satisfying the retained different-party
condition of the source protocol.

\subsection{Message-level separation}

Let one party $A$ originate $E_1=(A,\mathit{sid}_1,m_1)$ and
$E_2=(A,\mathit{sid}_2,m_2)$ in two Send occurrences. Fresh generation gives
$\mathit{sid}_1\neq\mathit{sid}_2$ and $m_1\neq m_2$. After batch creation,
the two collection rules take $E_1$ and $E_2$. The
\texttt{AdmitDistinctMessages} transition emits $\mathsf{Neq}(m_1,m_2)$ and
the batch event. The processing rules use the two corresponding origin facts
and emit ordered acceptance occurrences in the same batch context.

The existence lemma requires both Sends before admission and both accepts
after it; it does not order the two Sends relative to each other. Each exact
origin is accepted once in this batch, while the party coordinate is repeated.
Thus this is a witness against same-batch party distinction, not against the
message-level model's scoped occurrence injectivity.

\subsection{Party-level positive control and rejection limits}

For the positive control, two created parties $A_1\neq A_2$ each originate
one tuple. The same collection and processing sequence now takes
\texttt{AdmitDistinctParties}, emitting $\mathsf{Neq}(A_1,A_2)$.
The recorded \path{distinct_party_batch_exists} property binds both Sends
and both accepts to the displayed entries and one batch context. This is the
non-vacuity witness, not a guarantee that all admitted inputs complete.

The rejection branches are different evidence. A collected pair with equal
messages enables \texttt{RejectRepeatedMessage}; a pair with equal parties
enables \texttt{RejectRepeatedParty}. The recorded existence lemmas show that
these branches are reachable. Neither rejection lemma binds its preceding
Send tuple(s) to the rejected pair, and neither proves eventual rejection of
every invalid input. No more specific raw rejection witness is claimed here.
