\begin{abstract}
K-Waay's \batchreceive construction states that different inputs in one call
correspond to different parties. We study the semantic role of this condition
through a controlled comparison of three fixed-two-slot symbolic Tamarin
models: relaxed admission, message-level restriction, and party-level
admission. The models share a sender-origin and sequential processing
lifecycle, while their admission predicates constrain different identity
coordinates. In the recorded executions, relaxed admission has a witness in
which one matching sender origin supports two receiver-acceptance occurrences
in the same batch context. The message-level control satisfies message
distinction and scoped occurrence injectivity, yet two different messages
from one party remain accepted together. Party-level admission constrains the
collected party coordinates, establishes their distinction at the modeled
receiver-event boundary, and retains a reachable valid distinct-party batch.
The comparison separates message distinction and occurrence injectivity from
same-batch party distinction. It motivates an explicit participant relation
at the batch-composition boundary rather than treating message-level checks
as substitutes. The analysis assumes composition control, fresh symbolic
messages, and idealized origin matching. It provides no refinement theorem
for full K-Waay executions and establishes neither a cryptographic break nor
a deployed vulnerability. Results are drawn from existing execution records;
raw prover transcripts are not included.
\end{abstract}
